\begin{abstract}
Accurate simulation of human mobility is fundamental to resilient urban planning and the development of Digital Twins. However, current modeling approaches face a critical validity gap: deterministic baselines fail to capture the stochastic nature of individual choices, while emerging generative models often suffer from ``macroscopic shrinkage,'' producing diverse but physically unrealistic trajectories that underestimate urban velocity and displacement. Here, we propose a \textbf{Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion} framework that effectively reconciles microscopic stochasticity with macroscopic physical consistency. By decomposing trajectory generation into a deterministic low-frequency anchor and a stochastic high-frequency residual, our approach decouples the learning of physical scale from the modeling of uncertainty. We show on a city-scale Shenzhen taxi dataset that this hierarchical formulation resolves the shrinkage artifact, achieving state-of-the-art coverage (best-of-$K$) while restoring correct motion statistics. This work offers a scalable, data-driven engine for valid urban simulation, bridging the divide between deep generative modeling and transportation engineering.
\end{abstract}
