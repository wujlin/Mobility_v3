\begin{abstract}
Accurate simulation of human mobility is fundamental to resilient urban planning and mobility Digital Twins. However, current approaches face a validity gap: deterministic regression models miss stochastic route choice, while generative models can suffer from \emph{macroscopic shrinkage}, producing diverse yet physically unrealistic trajectories with under-estimated displacement and speed. We propose \textbf{Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion}, which decomposes trajectory generation into a frozen deterministic prior that captures macroscopic motion scale and a diffusion model for stochastic residuals, optionally conditioned on a train-only navigation field. Using a strict dt-fixed ($\Delta t=30$s), leakage-free protocol on the Shenzhen taxi dataset, we show that residual diffusion substantially improves macroscopic motion statistics while maintaining strong best-of-$K$ coverage. The resulting framework provides a practical path toward simulation-valid, data-driven urban mobility generation.
\end{abstract}
