Human mobility simulation supports urban analytics and transportation planning, and is increasingly discussed as a key component of mobility ``Digital Twins'' that enable scenario analysis under uncertainty \citep{Wang2022MobilityDigitalTwin}. In this setting, a trajectory generator should satisfy two requirements. First, it should represent \emph{stochasticity}: given the same observed history and origin--destination (OD) context, multiple plausible future trajectories may exist. Second, it should preserve \emph{physical realism} at the macroscopic scale: samples should maintain realistic displacement and speed statistics over the prediction horizon.

These requirements expose a gap in existing approaches. Deterministic regression models (e.g., L2) optimize point-wise error and typically approximate a conditional mean trajectory, which suppresses multi-modality and limits their use for risk-aware analysis. Generative models such as diffusion can produce diverse futures and achieve strong best-of-$K$ coverage, where best-of-$K$ reports the minimum error over $K$ sampled trajectories. However, when applied to city-scale mobility, diffusion samples can exhibit \textbf{macroscopic shrinkage}: trajectories systematically under-estimate net displacement and therefore yield biased macroscopic motion statistics, such as mean squared displacement (MSD; Eq.~\ref{eq:msd}) and radius of gyration (Rog; defined in Methodology).

We address the coverage--realism gap with \textbf{Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion}. The method decomposes future motion into (i) a frozen deterministic prior that captures OD-driven displacement scale and (ii) a diffusion model that learns stochastic residual deviations around that prior. In addition, we use a train-only navigation field---a non-parametric estimate of local mean flow computed from the training split---as a conditioning signal to improve route-level coverage without leaking information from validation/test data.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We build a dt-fixed ($\Delta t=30$s), leakage-free pipeline, including train-only normalization and navigation-field construction.
  \item We diagnose macroscopic shrinkage in pure diffusion under a strict evaluation protocol and propose residual diffusion as a structural remedy.
  \item We report micro-level errors and best-of-$K$ coverage together with macroscopic motion statistics (MSD/Rog) to evaluate simulation validity.
\end{itemize}
