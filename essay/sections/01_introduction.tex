Understanding and simulating human mobility is a cornerstone of modern urban engineering, underpinning applications from traffic signal timing to "Digital Twin" scenario analysis. For these systems to be effective, they require trajectory models that are **valid** simulations of reality—capturing not just the average flow, but the realistic stochasticity of driver choices.

However, existing modeling approaches face a critical validity gap. Classic deterministic models (e.g., L2 regression) effectively treat every driver as an optimal agent, collapsing all uncertainty into a single mean path. This behavior filters out the "tail events"—such as rare route choices or sudden deviations—that are precisely what risk-aware planners need to model. Conversely, emerging generative models (like pure diffusion) promise diversity but suffer from a severe physical artifact we explicitly diagnose in this work: **"macroscopic shrinkage."** While they generate diverse routes, the simulated vehicles systematically fail to maintain realistic speeds and displacements, rendering them useless for physical-world simulation where travel time accuracy is paramount.

To bridge this gap between **stochastic coverage** and **physical realism**, we propose a pragmatic, data-driven framework: Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion. Instead of relying on expensive road-graph engineering or complex kinematic constraints, we demonstrate that a simple residual decomposition—using a frozen deterministic baseline as a low-frequency anchor—can effectively calibrate the generative model. This provides a clear, low-cost path to upgrading traffic simulators from "mean-value estimators" to "physically consistent scenario generators."

\paragraph{Our approach and contributions.}
We develop a reproducible pipeline for \textbf{dt-fixed} trajectory generation ($\Delta t = 30$s) and compare:
(i) a deterministic L2 regression baseline,
(ii) a conditional diffusion trajectory generator \citep{ho2020ddpm},
and (iii) a physics-conditioned diffusion model that injects a \emph{navigation field} (a train-only, leakage-free local mean-flow prior) as additional conditioning.
We implement a strict data contract to prevent leakage (train-only normalization and nav\_field) and adopt an evaluation protocol for generative models including best-of-$K$, distribution metrics, and macroscopic motion statistics. We further provide a systematic diagnosis of the shrinkage issue and report ablations on training-time macro constraints.

The remainder of the report introduces the data and methods, presents quantitative and spatial results, and discusses limitations and future directions for city-scale mobility modeling.
