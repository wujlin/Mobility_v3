Understanding and simulating human mobility is a cornerstone of modern urban engineering, underpinning applications from traffic signal timing to mobility ``Digital Twins'' for scenario analysis and decision-making under uncertainty \citep{Wang2022MobilityDigitalTwin}. For such systems to be useful, trajectory generators must be \textbf{valid simulations} of reality---capturing both stochastic route choice and physically plausible motion.

Existing approaches face a critical validity gap. Deterministic regression (e.g., L2) collapses uncertainty into a single mean path, filtering out rare but important behaviors. In contrast, modern generative models (e.g., diffusion) provide diversity but can exhibit a severe artifact we explicitly diagnose: \textbf{macroscopic shrinkage}. Samples look multi-modal yet systematically under-estimate displacement and speed, limiting their value for engineering-grade simulation.

We address this gap with a pragmatic framework: \textbf{Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion}. The key idea is a residual decomposition that freezes a deterministic predictor as a low-frequency \emph{anchor} and lets diffusion model only the high-frequency stochastic \emph{residual}. We further introduce a train-only navigation-field prior as lightweight physics conditioning.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A strict dt-fixed ($\Delta t=30$s), leakage-free pipeline with train-only normalization and navigation-field construction.
  \item A diagnosis of macroscopic shrinkage in pure diffusion and a structural remedy via residual diffusion (prior + residual).
  \item An evaluation protocol for generative mobility models that jointly reports best-of-$K$ coverage and macroscopic motion statistics (MSD/Rog).
\end{itemize}
