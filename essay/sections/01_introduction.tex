Human mobility simulation supports urban analytics and transportation planning, and is increasingly discussed as a key component of mobility ``Digital Twins'' that enable scenario analysis under uncertainty \citep{Wang2022MobilityDigitalTwin}. Human mobility is typically goal-oriented; therefore, the origin--destination (OD) endpoints provide essential constraints on feasible future motion. At the same time, even under the same observed history and OD context, multiple plausible future trajectories may exist due to stochastic route choice and local driving variability. Accordingly, a trajectory generator should satisfy two requirements: (i) it should represent this conditional stochasticity, and (ii) it should preserve physical realism at the macroscopic scale, maintaining realistic displacement and speed statistics over the prediction horizon.

These requirements expose a gap in existing approaches. Deterministic regression models (e.g., L2) optimize point-wise error and typically approximate a conditional mean trajectory, which suppresses multi-modality and limits their use for risk-aware analysis. Generative models such as diffusion can produce diverse futures and are often evaluated via best-of-$K$ protocols to assess coverage. However, when applied to city-scale mobility, diffusion samples can exhibit \textbf{macroscopic shrinkage}: trajectories systematically under-estimate net displacement and therefore yield biased macroscopic motion statistics, such as mean squared displacement (MSD; displacement growth over time lag) and radius of gyration (Rog; spatial dispersion over a trajectory).

We address the coverage--realism gap with \textbf{Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion}. The method decomposes future motion into (i) a frozen deterministic prior that captures OD-driven displacement scale and (ii) a diffusion model that learns stochastic residual deviations around that prior. In addition, we use a train-only navigation field---a non-parametric estimate of local mean flow computed from the training split---as a conditioning signal to improve route-level coverage without leaking information from validation/test data.

We establish a leakage-free, dt-fixed ($\Delta t=30$s) pipeline for OD-conditioned trajectory generation, incorporating train-only normalization and navigation-field construction to ensure rigorous evaluation. Within this framework, we diagnose macroscopic shrinkage in pure diffusion models and propose physics-informed residual diffusion as a structural remedy that anchors motion scale via a deterministic prior. Our evaluation protocol goes beyond standard point-wise metrics, reporting micro-level best-of-$K$ coverage alongside macroscopic motion statistics (MSD and Rog) to comprehensively assess simulation validity in the context of mobility Digital Twins.

The remainder of the paper reviews related work, states the research question, describes the study region and data, details the methodology, and presents results and discussion.
