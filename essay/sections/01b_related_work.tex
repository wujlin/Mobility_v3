\subsection{Trajectory Prediction and Generation}
Trajectory prediction has traditionally been formulated as a deterministic sequence forecasting problem. Early approaches utilized kinematic filtering and social force models to capture interaction effects and feasibility constraints \citep{helbing1995social}. With the advent of deep learning, recurrent models (e.g., Social LSTM) became a standard approach for learning temporal dependencies from observed motion \citep{alahi2016social}. More recent works increasingly rely on Transformer-style architectures for long-range dependency modeling in motion forecasting \citep{zhou2022hivt,shi2022mtr}, and graph-based probabilistic predictors such as Trajectron++ for multi-agent forecasting \citep{salzmann2020trajectronpp}.

However, deterministic regression models inherently minimize the mean squared error (MSE), leading to predictions that collapse towards the conditional mean of the data distribution. In highly stochastic urban environments, this results in "average" trajectories that fail to capture the multi-modal nature of human mobility. To address this, generative models have gained prominence. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Conditional GANs have been widely applied to generate diverse trajectory samples \citep{gupta2018socialgan}. While successful in improving diversity, GANs often suffer from mode collapse and training instability, while VAEs may produce blurry samples due to the evidence lower bound objective.

\subsection{Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models}
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) have emerged as a powerful class of generative models with stable training objectives and strong sample quality \citep{ho2020ddpm}. Score-based diffusion models further generalize the framework via stochastic differential equations (SDEs) \citep{song2021sde}. A practical concern for trajectory generation is inference cost; numerical solvers and design refinements, such as DPM-Solver and EDM, significantly reduce the number of sampling steps while maintaining quality \citep{lu2022dpmsolver,karras2022edm}.

In mobility and motion forecasting, diffusion models have been explored for stochastic trajectory prediction and controllable generation, including Leapfrog Diffusion (LED) \citep{mao2023led}, diffusion-based GPS trajectory generation (DiffTraj) \citep{zhu2023difftraj}, and guidance-based controllable diffusion for joint prediction \citep{wang2024optimizingdiffusion}. Our work extends this direction by focusing on \emph{macroscopic physical consistency} in city-scale trajectory generation, and by diagnosing a ``shrinkage'' failure mode where generated trajectories under-estimate net displacement.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Deep Learning in Urban Mobility}
Integrating physical laws or domain knowledge into deep learning models---known as Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML)---is an active research frontier. In urban mobility, "physics" often refers to constraints imposed by road networks, traffic kinematics, or flow dynamics using partial differential equations (PDEs) or vector fields.

For road-network-aware generation, recent diffusion methods incorporate explicit structural constraints, e.g., graph-constrained diffusion for path planning \citep{shi2024gdp} and road-network-constrained trajectory generation \citep{wei2024diffrntraj}. Beyond mobility, diffusion models have also been extended with geometric and physical inductive biases, such as geometric trajectory diffusion \citep{han2024geotdm}, model-based diffusion for trajectory optimization \citep{pan2024mbdto}, and general physics-informed diffusion modeling \citep{bastek2025pidm}. Our approach adopts this philosophy in a lightweight and data-driven way: we explicitly condition the diffusion process on a non-parametric navigation field derived from historical trajectories, serving as a train-only mean-flow prior to guide denoising while keeping the v1 pipeline grid-based and reproducible.

At a macroscopic level, origin-destination (OD) flow modeling has been widely studied across transportation, geography, and computer science; see the interdisciplinary survey by \citet{rong2025odsurvey} for a broader perspective. Our focus here is micro-level OD-conditioned trajectory generation and its physical consistency.
