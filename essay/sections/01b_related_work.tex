\subsection{Simulation Validity Requirements in Mobility Digital Twins}
Transportation Digital Twins are commonly framed as connected cyber--physical systems that support scenario analysis through continual data integration and a service layer that links physical and digital components \citep{Nag2025ExploringDigitalTwinsTransportPlanning,Yan2023DigitalTwinTransportationInfrastructureManagement}. In this setting, trajectory generation is not only a forecasting task but also a component of the simulation engine: sampled trajectories serve as inputs to evaluate interventions and risks under uncertainty \citep{Wang2022MobilityDigitalTwin,FerreBigorra2022AdoptionUrbanDigitalTwins}. Consequently, model evaluation should go beyond point-wise accuracy and consider \emph{simulation validity}, including (i) stochastic coverage of plausible futures and (ii) physical realism of macroscopic motion statistics.

Traditional mobility simulation relies on Agent-Based Models (ABM) and hand-crafted kinematic rules \citep{helbing1995social}. These approaches are interpretable but often require extensive calibration to match city-scale heterogeneity. Data-driven models provide an alternative by learning mobility patterns directly from large trajectory logs. Early data-driven methods formulated trajectory prediction as deterministic regression using recurrent models or Transformers \citep{alahi2016social,zhou2022hivt,shi2022mtr}. While effective on mean error, deterministic regression typically produces a single conditional average trajectory, which under-represents multi-modality needed for scenario-level analysis.

Generative sequence models were introduced to recover multi-modality by sampling multiple plausible futures, including VAEs, GANs, and more recently diffusion models \citep{gupta2018socialgan,ho2020ddpm,zhu2023difftraj}. For Digital Twin applications, however, diversity alone is insufficient if sampled trajectories are not physically consistent at the macroscopic scale. This motivates methods that explicitly incorporate physical priors and evaluation protocols that jointly measure coverage and macroscopic realism.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Inductive Biases in Urban Computing}
Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) provides inductive biases to help data-driven mobility models respect urban dynamics. Existing work improves physical realism by incorporating explicit structure, such as graph priors and road-network-constrained generation \citep{shi2024gdp,wei2024diffrntraj}, or by introducing field- and PDE-inspired guidance signals \citep{bastek2025pidm}.

Our work focuses on \emph{macroscopic flow consistency} for simulation validity. We use a train-only navigation field as an empirical mean-flow conditioning signal and combine it with a residual decomposition so that macroscopic motion scale is anchored by a deterministic prior while diffusion focuses on stochastic deviations.
