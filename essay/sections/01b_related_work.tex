\subsection{Data-Driven Mobility Simulation for Digital Twins}
The development of urban Digital Twins requires high-fidelity simulation engines capable of reproducing the complex, stochastic behaviors of human mobility agents. Traditionally, mobility simulation has relied on Agent-Based Models (ABM) and microscopic kinematic rules \citep{helbing1995social}. While interpretable, these systems often require labor-intensive manual calibration of parameters and struggle to scale to city-wide heterogeneity. Consequently, data-driven approaches have emerged as a scalable alternative, learning agent behaviors directly from massive trajectory logs.

Early data-driven methods typically formulated trajectory prediction as a deterministic regression problem, utilizing recurrent architectures (RNNs, LSTMs) to model temporal dependencies \citep{alahi2016social}, or Transformer-based mechanisms to capture long-range interactions \citep{zhou2022hivt,shi2022mtr}. However, deterministic regression inherently minimizes point-wise mean error, leading to predictions that collapse towards a single conditional average. In the context of Digital Twins, this "mode collapse" is critical: it filters out the stochastic tail events—such as rare route choices or delays—that are essential for robust risk assessment and scenario planning.

To recover this lost stochasticity, generative sequence models have been increasingly adopted. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Conditional GANs have been applied to sample diverse future trajectories \citep{gupta2018socialgan}. More recently, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) have demonstrated superior capability in capturing complex, multi-modal distributions without the training instability of GANs \citep{ho2020ddpm,zhu2023difftraj}. Innovations such as Leapfrog Diffusion \citep{mao2023led} and guidance-based control \citep{wang2024optimizingdiffusion} further enhance the controllability of these generators. Yet, a persistent challenge in applying these generative models to city-scale simulation is \emph{physical validity}: generated trajectories frequently exhibit "macroscopic shrinkage" or kinematic inconsistencies, undermining the fidelity required for engineering-grade Digital Twins.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Inductive Biases in Urban Computing}
To ensure that data-driven models respect the physical laws of urban dynamics, Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) has become a key research frontier. In the context of mobility, "physics" refers to the constraints imposed by road network topology, traffic flow dynamics, and kinematic limits. 
Explicit structural constraints have been integrated into diffusion models via graph-based priors \citep{shi2024gdp} or road-network-constrained generation \citep{wei2024diffrntraj}. Beyond explicit constraints, inductive biases derived from vector fields or partial differential equations (PDEs) are also employed to guide the learning process \citep{bastek2025pidm}.

Our work aligns with this philosophy but adopts a specific focus on \emph{macroscopic flow consistency}. Rather than imposing rigid kinematic equations which may limit inference speed, we employ a non-parametric navigation field—derived from historical flow statistics—as a soft physical prior. This approach anchors the generative process in the empirical reality of the city's flow dynamics, providing a balanced solution for constructing the "behavioral layer" of an urban Digital Twin.
