\subsection{Trajectory Prediction and Generation}
Trajectory prediction has traditionally been formulated as a deterministic sequence forecasting problem. Early approaches utilized Kalman filters and social force models to capture kinematic constraints and inter-agent interactions \citep{helbing1995social}. With the advent of deep learning, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks became the standard for modeling temporal dependencies in mobility data \citep{alahi2016social}. More recent works have adopted Transformer-based architectures to improved long-range dependency modeling via self-attention mechanisms \citep{giuliari2021transformer}.

However, deterministic regression models inherently minimize the mean squared error (MSE), leading to predictions that collapse towards the conditional mean of the data distribution. In highly stochastic urban environments, this results in "average" trajectories that fail to capture the multi-modal nature of human mobility. To address this, generative models have gained prominence. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Conditional GANs have been widely applied to generate diverse trajectory samples \citep{gupta2018social}. While successful in improving diversity, GANs often suffer from mode collapse and training instability, while VAEs may produce blurry samples due to the evidence lower bound objective.

\subsection{Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models}
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) have recently emerged as a powerful class of generative models, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in image synthesis and increasing adoption in sequence modeling \citep{ho2020ddpm}. Unlike GANs, diffusion models offer stable training objectives based on likelihood maximization (or its variational bound) and high sample quality.
In the context of mobility, diffusion models have been applied to predict human trajectories and traffic flows. \cite{gu2022stochastic} proposed a diffusion-based stochastic trajectory prediction framework that outperforms CVAE and GAN baselines. Our work extends this direction by specifically addressing the macroscopic physical consistency of diffusion-generated trajectories in city-scale scenarios, identifying and mitigating the "shrinkage" phenomenon where generated paths lack sufficient directional persistence.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Deep Learning in Urban Mobility}
Integrating physical laws or domain knowledge into deep learning models—known as Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML)—is an active research frontier. In urban mobility, "physics" often refers to constraints imposed by road networks, traffic kinematics, or flow dynamics using partial differential equations (PDEs) or vector fields.
Recent works have explored using navigation fields or potential fields to guide trajectory prediction \citep{kothari2021interpretable}. By grounding the generation process in a learned or estimated physical vector field, models can achieve better generalization and adherence to environmental constraints. Our approach adopts this philosophy by explicitly conditioning the diffusion process on a non-parametric navigation field derived from historical data, effectively serving as a "mean-flow prior" to guide the denoising process.
