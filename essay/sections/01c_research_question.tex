Our starting point is a city-science question: if an urban region suffers a functional rupture, how does that rupture imprint itself on everyday mobility? We approach this through cross-city transfer and a behavioral baseline.

\paragraph{Main research question.}
\textbf{Can we detect and localize urban functional ruptures by comparing observed mobility in a target city to a learned behavioral reference frame trained on functional cities?}

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
We pre-register three hypotheses that connect the modeling design to the scientific claims:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1 (Behavioral regularity is transferable):} route-choice patterns learned from one functional city generalize to other functional cities with small residuals, indicating that ``normal'' detour behavior is not purely city-idiosyncratic.
  \item \textbf{H2 (Rupture signature):} in Detroit, the spatial distribution of residuals between the behavioral reference and realized mobility correlates with independent rupture indicators (e.g., vacancy, safety, or service accessibility proxies).
  \item \textbf{H3 (Behavioral $>$ physical reference):} a behavioral reference frame produces rupture signatures that are more spatially localized and more correlated with independent rupture indicators than a physical reference frame (shortest-path baseline). This validates that behavioral references separate normal trade-offs from dysfunction-driven avoidance.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Role of model structure and diffusion.}
The model is not a goal in itself; it serves to instantiate a behavioral reference that makes the research question answerable at the level of corridors and maps. We therefore treat known generator failure modes (e.g., overly direct routes in end-to-end generators) as threats to reference validity, and adopt decision-consistent planning to mitigate them. Diffusion is considered only after a valid single-mode behavioral baseline is established, and is used to represent multi-modal normal route choice rather than to ``fix'' infeasibility.
