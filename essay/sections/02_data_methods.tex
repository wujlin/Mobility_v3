\subsection{Notation and Problem Setup}
We study \textbf{OD-conditioned trajectory generation with a known destination} (KnownDestination). Trajectories are represented in a grid coordinate system. Let $\mathbf{p}_t \in \mathbb{R}^2$ denote the 2D position at step $t$ (grid indices in $[y,x]$ order), and define step displacement (velocity) as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_t = \mathbf{p}_{t+1} - \mathbf{p}_t .
  \label{eq:vel_def}
\end{equation}
We use a fixed sampling interval $\Delta t = 30$s (dt-fixed), so $\mathbf{v}_t$ has explicit time semantics and is proportional to physical velocity.
We use an observation window length $H=8$ and prediction horizon $F=12$ (4 minutes of history and 6 minutes of future). Denote the observed history as $\mathbf{X}=\{(\mathbf{p}_t,\mathbf{v}_t)\}_{t=-H+1}^{0}$ and the future velocities as $\mathbf{Y}=\{\mathbf{v}_t\}_{t=1}^{F}$.
Given a global context vector $\mathbf{c}$ (time features and OD endpoints), our goal is to model the conditional distribution
\begin{equation}
  p_\theta(\mathbf{Y}\mid \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{c}).
  \label{eq:cond_dist}
\end{equation}
At inference, we sample $K$ future trajectories (by sampling $K$ future velocity sequences) to represent multi-modality.

\subsection{Data and Preprocessing (dt-fixed)}
We use the Shenzhen taxicab GPS dataset \citep{zhang2015urbancps}. We preprocess trajectories into grid space and enforce a strict, leakage-free data contract.

\paragraph{Grid rasterization.}
We discretize the city bounding box (latitude $[22.45, 22.85]$, longitude $[113.75, 114.65]$) into a $H_g \times W_g = 400 \times 800$ grid. Positions are stored as grid coordinates $\mathbf{p}_t=[y_t,x_t]$.

\paragraph{dt-fixed resampling.}
We resample each trajectory to a fixed interval $\Delta t=30$s and drop segments with large gaps or insufficient length. After resampling, the split sizes are train=97,222, val=21,103, test=21,309 trajectories.

\paragraph{Leakage-free strict products.}
All normalization statistics and the navigation field (Section~\ref{sec:navfield}) are computed using the \textbf{train split only}.
This prevents information leakage from validation/test into priors or scaling constants.

\paragraph{Normalization and conditioning.}
We normalize positions and velocities using train-only statistics. The global conditioning vector includes (i) time features (hour-of-day, day-of-week) and (ii) origin and destination (OD) endpoints, both normalized in the same coordinate system.

\paragraph{Deterministic baseline (L2 regression).}
The baseline predicts a single future velocity sequence by minimizing L2 error. As a deterministic model, it corresponds to $K=1$ at inference.

\paragraph{Conditional diffusion trajectory generator.}
We adopt a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) \citep{ho2020ddpm} to sample future velocity sequences conditioned on history and OD context.

\paragraph{Physics-conditioned diffusion (navigation field).}
We extend the diffusion model by injecting an additional, non-parametric prior in the form of a navigation-field patch around the current position (Section~\ref{sec:navfield}).

\paragraph{Residual diffusion (v1.1, frozen deterministic prior).}
In Phase B we observe a systematic ``shrinkage'' failure mode in pure diffusion: sampled trajectories tend to under-estimate macroscopic displacement (low MSD/Rog). To decouple low-frequency scale from stochastic variation, we adopt a residual formulation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{1:F} = \mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{1:F} + \mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{res}}_{1:F},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{1:F} = f_{\mathrm{base}}(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{c}),
  \label{eq:residual_decomp}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\mathrm{base}}$ is a frozen deterministic baseline (trained under the same dt-fixed protocol), and the diffusion model learns the conditional distribution of residual velocities $p_\theta(\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{res}} \mid \mathbf{X},\mathbf{c})$. At inference, we sample residuals and add them back to the prior to obtain full future velocities.

\subsection{Conditional DDPM for Future Velocities}
\label{sec:ddpm}
We model the clean future velocity sequence as a matrix $\mathbf{y}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{F\times 2}$, and define a forward noising process that gradually perturbs $\mathbf{y}_0$ into $\mathbf{y}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$.
Using a variance schedule $\{\beta_t\}_{t=1}^T$ with $\alpha_t=1-\beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t=\prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$, the forward process admits the closed form
\begin{equation}
  q(\mathbf{y}_t \mid \mathbf{y}_0)=\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{y}_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\mathbf{y}_0,\; (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I}\right).
  \label{eq:ddpm_forward}
\end{equation}
The reverse process is learned with a noise predictor $\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{y}_t,t,\mathbf{c})$ and optimized using the simplified objective
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}} =
  \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{y}_0,t,\epsilon}\left[\left\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{y}_t,t,\mathbf{c})\right\|_2^2\right],
  \label{eq:ddpm_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$ and $\mathbf{y}_t=\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\mathbf{y}_0+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Reverse diffusion sampling (future velocities)}
  \label{alg:ddpm_sampling}
  \begin{algpseudocode}[1]
    \Require history $\mathbf{X}$, context $\mathbf{c}$, horizon $F$, diffusion steps $T$
    \State sample $\mathbf{y}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$
    \For{$t = T, T\!-\!1, \dots, 1$}
      \State $\epsilon \leftarrow \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{y}_t, t, \mathbf{c})$
      \State $\mathbf{y}_{t-1} \leftarrow \mathrm{DDPM\_STEP}(\mathbf{y}_t, \epsilon, t)$
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $\mathbf{y}_0$ (sampled future velocities)
  \end{algpseudocode}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Navigation Field Prior and Local Patching}
\label{sec:navfield}
We construct a train-only navigation field $\mathcal{F}$ that captures a coarse-grained mean flow of urban mobility in grid space. For each grid cell $(i,j)$, let $\mathcal{K}_{ij}$ be the set of training timesteps whose position falls into that cell. We estimate the mean step displacement and its direction as
\begin{align}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij} &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{ij}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{ij}} \mathbf{v}_k,
  &
  \mathbf{u}_{ij} &= \frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}}{\|\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}\|_2 + \varepsilon},
  \label{eq:nav_direction}
\end{align}
and record the mean speed $s_{ij}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{ij}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{ij}} \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_2$ along with the visit count $n_{ij}=|\mathcal{K}_{ij}|$.

At inference time, we crop a local patch around the last observed position $\mathbf{p}_0$:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\mathbf{p}_0) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times K\times K}, \quad
  \Phi = [\,\mathbf{u}_y,\mathbf{u}_x, s\,],
  \label{eq:nav_patch}
\end{equation}
where $K=32$ in our experiments. The patch is encoded by a lightweight CNN to produce an embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{nav}}$, which is concatenated with the global condition. Speed values are normalized by a train-only constant (\texttt{nav\_max\_speed}) to keep the patch scale stable.

\subsection{Training-Time Macro Regularization (Ablation)}
\label{sec:macro_reg}
To mitigate the macroscopic ``shrinkage'' phenomenon (under-estimated net displacement), we explore an optional macro regularizer. Given $\mathbf{y}_t$ and the predicted noise $\epsilon_\theta$, we reconstruct an estimate of the clean sample
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{y}}_0 = \frac{\mathbf{y}_t - \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{y}_t,t,\mathbf{c})}{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} + 10^{-8}}.
  \label{eq:ddpm_x0_recon}
\end{equation}
We then compute the implied positions by summing displacements (Eq.~\ref{eq:vel_def}) and apply an end-to-end displacement penalty:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\mathbf{p}}_k &= \mathbf{p}_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \hat{\mathbf{v}}_i, \quad \hat{\mathbf{v}} \in \hat{\mathbf{y}}_0, \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{macro}} &= \left\|(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_F - \mathbf{p}_0) - (\mathbf{p}_F - \mathbf{p}_0)\right\|_2^2.
  \label{eq:macro_loss}
\end{align}
To avoid applying geometric constraints at highly noisy diffusion timesteps, we gate this loss to early timesteps (higher signal-to-noise ratio): $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}} + \lambda \,\mathbb{I}[t < \tau]\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{macro}}$.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Geographic-space Visualization (Scheme B)}
We evaluate on the test split using both micro-level and macro-level metrics. For generative models we draw $K=20$ samples and report both mean and best-of-$K$ (oracle) scores.

\paragraph{Micro metrics.}
We report ADE/FDE for each condition, and also compute discrete Fr\'echet distance and DTW as distribution-aware trajectory-shape measures.
For ADE/FDE, we summarize \textbf{mean} over samples and \textbf{best-of-$K$} (minimum over samples) to quantify coverage.

\paragraph{Macro metrics.}
We report mean squared displacement (MSD) curves and radius of gyration (Rog) to characterize low-frequency motion. MSD is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MSD}(\tau) = \left\langle \left\|\mathbf{p}_{t+\tau} - \mathbf{p}_t\right\|_2^2 \right\rangle_{t,\;\mathrm{conditions}},
  \label{eq:msd}
\end{equation}
and Rog is computed per trajectory as $\mathrm{Rog}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{F}\sum_{t=1}^{F}\|\mathbf{p}_t-\bar{\mathbf{p}}\|_2^2}$.

\paragraph{Geographic-space visualization (Scheme B).}
Models operate in grid space, but for urban interpretation we map grid coordinates back to latitude/longitude by a linear bbox projection using the recorded bbox and grid shape. This produces ``map-like'' overlays and density plots (without road-level map matching). Depending on the grid convention, the latitude axis may require a $y$-flip to match a north-up view; we follow the same convention as our visualization scripts and report this mapping as an approximate display transform.
