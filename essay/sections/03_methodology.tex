This section introduces the OD-conditioned generation task, the compared model families, and the physics-informed residual formulation.

\subsection{Problem Setup}
Trajectories are represented in a grid coordinate system. Let $\mathbf{p}_t \in \mathbb{R}^2$ denote the 2D position at step $t$ (grid indices in $[y,x]$ order), and define step displacement (velocity) as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_t = \mathbf{p}_{t+1} - \mathbf{p}_t .
  \label{eq:vel_def}
\end{equation}
With dt-fixed sampling, $\mathbf{v}_t$ has explicit temporal meaning.
We denote the observed history as $\mathbf{X}=\{(\mathbf{p}_t,\mathbf{v}_t)\}_{t=-H+1}^{0}$ and the future velocities as $\mathbf{Y}=\{\mathbf{v}_t\}_{t=1}^{F}$.
Given a global context vector $\mathbf{c}$ (time features and OD endpoints), our goal is to model the conditional distribution
\begin{equation}
  p_\theta(\mathbf{Y}\mid \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{c}).
  \label{eq:cond_dist}
\end{equation}
In our experiments, we use $H=8$ observed steps and $F=12$ future steps under dt-fixed $\Delta t=30$s.
Given the last observed position $\mathbf{p}_0$, predicted positions are obtained by cumulative summation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_k = \mathbf{p}_0 + \sum_{t=1}^{k}\mathbf{v}_t,\quad k=1,\dots,F.
  \label{eq:pos_from_vel}
\end{equation}
We use \emph{macroscopic shrinkage} to refer to systematic under-estimation of net displacement $\|\mathbf{p}_F-\mathbf{p}_0\|_2$ and mean speed $\frac{1}{F}\sum_{t=1}^{F}\|\mathbf{v}_t\|_2$, which manifests as biased macroscopic motion statistics such as MSD and Rog.

\subsection{Model Framework}
We compare a deterministic prior (L2 regression), conditional diffusion generators, and our residual formulation that explicitly separates macroscopic scale from stochastic variability (Figure~\ref{fig:method-overview}).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/fig1_framework.png}
  \caption{Framework overview. A frozen deterministic prior captures OD-driven macroscopic motion scale. A diffusion model samples stochastic residual deviations, optionally conditioned on a train-only navigation field. The sum yields diverse trajectories while preserving macroscopic motion statistics.}
  \label{fig:method-overview}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Deterministic prior (L2 regression).}
We train an L2 regression model as a deterministic predictor, and use the same model as the \emph{deterministic prior} in residual variants. The prior predicts a single future velocity sequence,
$\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{1:F}=f_{\mathrm{prior}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{c})$, and is frozen when training diffusion residuals (equivalently $K=1$ at inference).

\paragraph{Conditional diffusion generator.}
We adopt a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) \citep{ho2020ddpm} to sample a target sequence conditioned on $(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{c})$. In the non-residual setting the target is the future velocity sequence; in residual variants the target is the residual velocity sequence defined below. The model is trained to predict diffusion noise with the standard objective
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}} =
  \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{y}_0,t,\epsilon}\left[\left\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{y}_t,t,\mathbf{c})\right\|_2^2\right],
  \label{eq:ddpm_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{y}_0$ is the clean target velocity matrix (future velocities in non-residual variants, residual velocities in residual variants), $\mathbf{y}_t$ is its noisy version at timestep $t$, and $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})$.

\paragraph{Navigation field conditioning (train-only).}
To incorporate an empirical flow signal without relying on a road graph, we construct a \emph{navigation field} that summarizes local mean displacement direction and speed in grid space. Importantly, this field is computed using the \textbf{training split only} and is fixed during training and evaluation to satisfy the leakage-free data contract.
For each grid cell $(i,j)$ we estimate the mean displacement $\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}$ over training steps that fall into the cell, the corresponding mean direction $\mathbf{u}_{ij}$, and mean speed $s_{ij}$:
\begin{align}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij} &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{ij}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}_{ij}} \mathbf{v}_k, &
  \mathbf{u}_{ij} &= \frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}}{\|\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{ij}\|_2 + \delta}, &
  s_{ij} &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{ij}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{T}_{ij}} \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_2,
  \label{eq:nav_field_def}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{T}_{ij}$ is the set of training steps located in cell $(i,j)$ and $\delta$ is a small constant for numerical stability. At inference, we crop a local patch around the last observed position $\mathbf{p}_0$:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\mathbf{p}_0) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times P\times P}, \quad
  \Phi = [\,u_y, u_x, s\,],
  \label{eq:nav_patch}
\end{equation}
where $u_y$ and $u_x$ are the two components of the direction field $\mathbf{u}=[u_y,u_x]$. We encode the patch with a CNN encoder and concatenate the resulting embedding with the global condition. In our experiments, we use $P=32$.
We refer to a diffusion generator augmented with navigation field conditioning as \emph{Physics} in our results for brevity.

\paragraph{Main method: Physics-Informed Residual Diffusion.}
Pure diffusion can exhibit \emph{macroscopic shrinkage} (Eq.~\ref{eq:pos_from_vel}), where the sampled net displacement scale is systematically smaller than the ground truth. We address this structurally by decomposing future motion into a deterministic prior plus a stochastic residual:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_{1:F} = \mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{1:F} + \mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{res}}_{1:F},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{prior}}_{1:F} = f_{\mathrm{prior}}(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{c}),
  \label{eq:residual_decomp}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\mathrm{prior}}$ is the frozen deterministic prior trained under the same dt-fixed protocol. During training, we define the diffusion target as the residual velocity $\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{res}}=\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{prior}}$, so the model learns stochastic deviations around the prior rather than the full displacement scale. During inference, we sample $\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{res}}$ and add it back to the deterministic prior to obtain the final future trajectory.

\paragraph{Destination guidance via classifier-free guidance (CFG).}
In the KnownDestination setting, destination is a legitimate conditioning input, but its influence can be too weak (leading to conservative motion) or too strong (over-constraining stochasticity). We therefore adopt classifier-free guidance (CFG) as an \emph{inference-time} control knob.
During training we randomly drop the destination coordinates in the condition vector with probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ to form an ``unconditional'' branch. At sampling time, we evaluate both conditional and unconditional predictions and combine them as
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\epsilon} = \hat{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{u}} + s\cdot(\hat{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{c}}-\hat{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{u}}),
  \label{eq:cfg}
\end{equation}
where $s$ is the CFG scale, and $\hat{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{c}}$/$\hat{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{u}}$ denote conditional/unconditional noise predictions. In this essay we treat CFG as a protocolized knob and report two interpretable settings ($s=2$ and $s=3$) rather than an open-ended sweep.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
For generative models we draw $K$ samples per condition and report both mean errors and best-of-$K$ (oracle; minimum over $K$ samples) metrics to quantify coverage. We evaluate micro-level errors using average displacement error (ADE) and final displacement error (FDE) computed on predicted position sequences:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{ADE} &= \frac{1}{F}\sum_{t=1}^{F}\left\|\mathbf{p}_t-\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{gt}}_t\right\|_2, &
  \mathrm{FDE} &= \left\|\mathbf{p}_F-\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{gt}}_F\right\|_2.
  \label{eq:ade_fde}
\end{align}
For generative models, mean ADE/FDE average over samples, and best-of-$K$ takes the minimum over the $K$ sampled trajectories for each condition.

We evaluate macro-level physical realism via mean squared displacement (MSD) curves and radius of gyration (Rog). MSD is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MSD}(\tau) = \left\langle \left\|\mathbf{p}_{t+\tau} - \mathbf{p}_t\right\|_2^2 \right\rangle,
  \label{eq:msd}
\end{equation}
and Rog is computed per trajectory as $\mathrm{Rog}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{F}\sum_{t=1}^{F}\|\mathbf{p}_t-\bar{\mathbf{p}}\|_2^2}$, where $\bar{\mathbf{p}}$ is the mean position over the prediction horizon.
