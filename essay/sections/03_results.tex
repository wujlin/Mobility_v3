\paragraph{Micro-level errors.}
The deterministic baseline achieves the best mean errors (ADE\_mean=5.467, FDE\_mean=8.855; Figure~\ref{fig:micro}). This is expected because it is optimized to predict a single conditional mean trajectory.
In contrast, diffusion-based generators have higher mean errors (Diffusion ADE\_mean=6.741, Physics ADE\_mean=6.744), but exhibit substantially better \emph{best-of-$K$} capability:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Diffusion: ADE\_best=2.836, FDE\_best=3.950
  \item Physics: ADE\_best=2.510, FDE\_best=3.419
\end{itemize}
This indicates that the generative models can place plausible futures among their samples even when the averaged prediction is less accurate.

\paragraph{Distribution-aware metrics.}
Diffusion/Physics show larger Fr\'echet and DTW than the deterministic baseline in this setting, reflecting a trade-off between distributional diversity and point-wise mean accuracy.

\paragraph{Macro-level physical realism: shrinkage remains.}
We observe a clear shrinkage phenomenon in macroscopic motion statistics. Compared to the ground truth:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Diffusion has Rog=3.056 (0.582$\times$GT\_Rog=5.247) and MSD\_10=100.948 (0.289$\times$GT\_MSD\_10=349.740).
  \item Physics improves slightly to Rog=3.435 (0.655$\times$GT) and MSD\_10=116.869 (0.334$\times$GT), but still underestimates net displacement.
\end{itemize}
Thus, while physics conditioning helps directional guidance and best-of-$K$, it does not fully resolve low-frequency displacement underestimation.
Figure~\ref{fig:msd} visualizes this gap directly via MSD curves over real time lags ($\tau = k\Delta t$).

\subsection{Ablation: training-time macro loss (EPE with batch normalization)}
Motivated by expert feedback, we tested a training-time macro constraint based on end-to-end displacement error (EPE) with (i) hard timestep gating ($t < 0.5T$) to avoid high-noise shortcuts, and (ii) batch-level normalization to prevent instability on low-displacement windows.
On a large validation subset (K=1, 51,200 conditions), sweeping $\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.03, 0.06\}$ produced nearly identical results, suggesting a plateau region where the macro signal is not yet strong enough to change the learned dynamics. Macro shrinkage improves modestly but remains substantial (e.g., pred\_speed/gt\_speed $\approx 0.60$; MSD\_10/GT\_MSD\_10 $\approx 0.26$).

\subsection{Geographic-space visualization (urban interpretation)}
To connect model behavior to urban spatial structure, we visualize generated trajectories in geographic space by mapping grid coordinates back to latitude/longitude using the dataset bounding box. Figure~\ref{fig:geo-overlay} shows trajectory overlays (subset of saved evaluation samples), and Figure~\ref{fig:geo-density} shows spatial density maps (predicted density with ground-truth contours). These ``map-like'' visualizations highlight two practical aspects:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Spatial heterogeneity}: mobility concentrates along specific corridors and regions rather than being spatially uniform.
  \item \textbf{Probabilistic futures}: diffusion-based generators yield a family of plausible continuations, which is more aligned with complex and stochastic urban dynamics than a single mean path.
\end{itemize}
