This section will report results once the cross-city behavioral reference is instantiated (reference cities selected and transfer audited). Results will be organized around the hypotheses in Section~3 and the rupture-signature outputs (residual maps), not around point-wise prediction error alone.

We refer to the spatialized residual map as a \emph{behavioral avoidance field}: locations and corridors that the behavioral reference predicts should be traversed under normal trade-offs but are systematically under-realized in Detroit.

\subsection{H1: Functional$\to$Functional Transfer Audit}
We will evaluate transfer between two functional cities to establish that the learned baseline represents normal route choice rather than city-specific idiosyncrasies. The key output is a low residual baseline for functional$\to$functional transfer.

\subsection{H2: Detroit Rupture Signatures}
We will visualize residual maps over Detroit and quantify where predicted normal corridors diverge from realized mobility. We will report which residual components dominate (detour magnitude, corridor shifts, or destination-side approach errors) and how these align with known spatial heterogeneity.

\subsection{H3: Behavioral vs.\ Physical Reference}
We will compare residuals derived from the behavioral reference to residuals derived from a shortest-path baseline. The goal is to show that the behavioral reference separates normal detours from rupture-related avoidance more effectively.

\subsection{External Validation and Ablations}
We will validate residual hotspots against independent indicators (e.g., vacancy, safety, and service accessibility proxies) and conduct ablations on context sources (soft map prior, POI semantics, overhead imagery) and model structure (autoregressive planning). Diffusion will be evaluated only after a stable single-mode reference is established, to isolate its role in multi-modal normal route choice rather than feasibility repair.
