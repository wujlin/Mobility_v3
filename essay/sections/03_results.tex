\paragraph{Micro-level errors and coverage.}
The deterministic baseline achieves the best mean errors (Table~\ref{tab:phaseb_quick}), which is expected for L2 regression that approximates the conditional mean.
In contrast, diffusion-based generators have higher mean errors but much stronger \textbf{best-of-$K$} capability: Physics improves the oracle upper bound over data-only diffusion (ADE$_\text{best}$ 2.510 vs. 2.836; FDE$_\text{best}$ 3.419 vs. 3.950), indicating better coverage when the navigation-field prior is provided.

\paragraph{Macro-level physical realism: shrinkage persists.}
Despite strong best-of-$K$, diffusion and physics-conditioned diffusion under-estimate macroscopic motion. For example, diffusion has Rog=3.056 compared to GT\_Rog=5.247 (ratio $\approx 0.58$), and MSD$_{10}$ is 100.948 compared to GT\_MSD$_{10}$=349.740 (ratio $\approx 0.29$).
Physics conditioning improves these macro statistics modestly (Rog=3.435, MSD$_{10}$=116.869) but does not eliminate the gap.

\subsection{Ablation: Training-Time Macro Loss (EPE)}
Motivated by the shrinkage phenomenon, we tested a training-time macroscopic constraint based on end-to-end displacement error (Eq.~\ref{eq:macro_loss}). To avoid shortcut solutions at highly noisy diffusion timesteps, we apply hard gating (only for early timesteps) and use batch-level normalization for stability.
Empirically, macro supervision is \emph{sensitive}: if the weight is too small, improvements can plateau; if too large, the model may over-compensate and produce heavy-tailed motion that degrades \emph{typical} samples (K=1), even if oracle best-of-$K$ improves. This motivates a structural fix rather than further exhaustive $\lambda$ sweeps.

\subsection{Residual Diffusion (v1.1): Frozen Prior + Stochastic Residual}
Given the limited gains from training-time macro loss, we adopt a residual formulation (Eq.~\ref{eq:residual_decomp}) that uses a frozen deterministic baseline as a low-frequency prior and learns stochastic residuals with diffusion.
Table~\ref{tab:phaseb_residual_test_fast} summarizes a fast, leakage-free check on the test split (K=10) using the deterministic baseline checkpoint (\texttt{baseline\_b\_dt30}) as a frozen prior. Residual diffusion largely restores macroscopic scale (speed and Rog ratios close to 1) while maintaining strong oracle best-of-$K$ capability. Residual \emph{physics} diffusion further improves the oracle upper bound (best-of-$K$) but remains slightly conservative in MSD/Rog due to the mean-flow tether effect of the navigation-field prior.
Unless otherwise stated, we keep the sampling schedule consistent with training (100 diffusion steps) and use smaller subsets (\texttt{max\_batches}) only for quick validation; aggressive step-reduction can change calibration and is not used for reporting.
