\subsection{Quantitative Results (Phase B)}
We evaluate the models on the test split using both micro-level accuracy metrics and macro-level physical consistency metrics. Table 1 summarizes the performance of the Baseline, Diffusion, and Physics-conditioned Diffusion models.

\paragraph{Micro-level Performance}
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:micro}, the deterministic baseline achieves the lowest mean errors (ADE: 5.47, FDE: 8.86). This is an expected property of regression models trained with L2 loss, which tend to predict the conditional mean of the future distribution. However, this comes at the cost of diversity ($K=1$).
Generative models, evaluated with $K=20$, show higher mean errors but significantly better \textbf{Best-of-K} performance (Physics ADE\_best: 2.51, Diffusion ADE\_best: 2.84). This confirms that while individual samples may vary, the generative models cover the ground truth distribution more effectively than a single deterministic forecast.

\paragraph{Macro-level Analysis: The Shrinkage Phenomenon}
Despite their coverage advantage, generative models exhibit a systematic "shrinkage" in macroscopic motion statistics. The average Radius of Gyration (Rog) for Diffusion is 3.06, compared to the Ground Truth (GT) of 5.25 (Ratio $\approx 0.58$). Similarly, the Mean Squared Displacement at 10 steps (MSD\_10) is only $\approx 29\%$ of the GT value.
Figure~\ref{fig:msd} presents the MSD curves ($\text{MSD}(\tau)$ vs. lag $\tau$). The GT curve follows a super-diffusive trend ($\text{MSD} \propto \tau^\alpha$ with $\alpha > 1$), characteristic of directed travel. In contrast, the uncalibrated generative models show a flatter slope, resembling a constrained random walk. This indicates a lack of \textbf{directional persistence} in the learned dynamics: generated trajectories change direction too frequently, cancelling out net displacement.

\subsection{Ablation Study: Macro Regularization}
To address the shrinkage issue, we investigated training-time macroscopic regularization. We introduced an end-to-end displacement penalty (EPE) applied during training.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to Regularization Weight}
We swept the regularization weight $\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.1\}$. Results indicate a critical trade-off:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Low $\lambda$ (< 0.01)}: The macro signal is too weak compared to the primary diffusion denoising loss, resulting in negligible change in Rog/MSD.
    \item \textbf{High $\lambda$ (> 0.05)}: The model fails to converge to realistic trajectories. Instead of producing smooth, directed paths, the model minimizes the EPE loss by introducing \textbf{high-frequency jitter}—pathological oscillations that increase the total path length and "spread" without semantic correctness. This "noise explosion" phenomenon suggests that imposing geometric constraints on noisy intermediate states (large diffusion timesteps) can be detrimental.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Timestep Gating}
We further experimented with gating the macro loss, applying it only when the diffusion timestep $t < T_{thr}$. Setting $T_{thr}=50$ (halfway point) effectively suppresses the jitter artifact, as the geometric constraint is only applied when the trajectory structure is semi-formed. However, even with gating, the recovery of Rog is partial ($\approx 65\%$ of GT), indicating that the "mean-reversion" bias of the diffusion objective is a strong attractor that is difficult to fully overcome with auxiliary losses derived from noisy proxies.
