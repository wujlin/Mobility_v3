\subsection{Coverage vs.\ Physical Realism (dt-fixed=30s)}
We evaluate on a strict dt-fixed ($\Delta t=30$s) protocol using 320 sampled test conditions. The deterministic baseline is evaluated with $K=1$, while diffusion-based generators use $K=20$ samples per condition.

\paragraph{Micro-level errors and coverage.}
As expected, the deterministic baseline achieves the lowest mean errors (ADE$_\text{mean}=5.467$, FDE$_\text{mean}=8.855$). Diffusion improves multi-modality: best-of-$K$ (oracle; minimum error over $K$ samples) errors are substantially lower (Diffusion ADE$_\text{best}=2.836$; Physics ADE$_\text{best}=2.510$), indicating stronger coverage when the navigation-field prior is provided.

\paragraph{Macro-level physical realism: shrinkage in pure diffusion.}
Despite strong best-of-$K$, pure diffusion under-estimates macroscopic motion (e.g., Rog=3.056 vs.\ GT\_Rog=5.247; MSD$_{10}$=100.948 vs.\ GT\_MSD$_{10}$=349.740). Physics conditioning improves these statistics modestly (Rog=3.435; MSD$_{10}$=116.869) but does not eliminate the gap. Figure~\ref{fig:phaseb_quant} visualizes this pattern via micro summaries and MSD curves.

\subsection{Main Result: Residual Diffusion Mitigates Shrinkage}
To separate macroscopic motion scale from stochastic variation, we apply residual diffusion (Eq.~\ref{eq:residual_decomp}), using a frozen deterministic baseline as a prior and learning diffusion residuals. Table~\ref{tab:residual_test} reports a leakage-free evaluation on 51,200 test conditions ($K=10$): residual diffusion restores macroscopic scale (speed and Rog ratios close to 1) while retaining strong best-of-$K$ behavior. Residual \emph{physics} diffusion further improves best-of-$K$, but its macroscopic statistics remain slightly conservative, consistent with conditioning on a train-only mean-flow prior.
