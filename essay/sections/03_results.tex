\paragraph{Micro-level errors and coverage.}
The deterministic baseline achieves the best mean errors (Table~\ref{tab:phaseb_quick}), which is expected for L2 regression that approximates the conditional mean.
In contrast, diffusion-based generators have higher mean errors but much stronger \textbf{best-of-$K$} capability: Physics improves the oracle upper bound over data-only diffusion (ADE$_\text{best}$ 2.510 vs. 2.836; FDE$_\text{best}$ 3.419 vs. 3.950), indicating better coverage when the navigation-field prior is provided.

\paragraph{Macro-level physical realism: shrinkage persists.}
Despite strong best-of-$K$, diffusion and physics-conditioned diffusion under-estimate macroscopic motion. For example, diffusion has Rog=3.056 compared to GT\_Rog=5.247 (ratio $\approx 0.58$), and MSD$_{10}$ is 100.948 compared to GT\_MSD$_{10}$=349.740 (ratio $\approx 0.29$).
Physics conditioning improves these macro statistics modestly (Rog=3.435, MSD$_{10}$=116.869) but does not eliminate the gap.

\subsection{Ablation: Training-Time Macro Loss (EPE)}
Motivated by the shrinkage phenomenon, we tested a training-time macroscopic constraint based on end-to-end displacement error (Eq.~\ref{eq:macro_loss}). To avoid shortcut solutions at highly noisy diffusion timesteps, we apply hard gating (only for early timesteps) and use batch-level normalization for stability.
On a large validation subset (51,200 conditions, $K=1$), sweeping $\lambda \in \{0.01, 0.03, 0.06\}$ produced nearly identical results, suggesting a plateau region where the macro signal is not yet strong enough to change the learned low-frequency dynamics. Macro shrinkage improves modestly but remains substantial (e.g., pred\_speed/gt\_speed $\approx 0.60$; MSD$_{10}$/GT\_MSD$_{10}$ $\approx 0.26$).
