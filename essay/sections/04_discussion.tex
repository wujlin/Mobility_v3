\paragraph{Micro--macro tension in generative mobility modeling.}
Our results highlight a consistent trade-off: deterministic regression minimizes mean error but collapses multi-modality, while diffusion improves best-of-$K$ coverage yet can violate macroscopic realism. In our dt-fixed setting, this manifests as \emph{macroscopic shrinkage}---samples that look plausible locally but systematically under-estimate displacement and speed.

\paragraph{Why shrinkage happens and what physics conditioning changes.}
Iterative denoising can favor conservative updates when direction is ambiguous, leading to accumulated loss of directional persistence. The navigation-field prior helps by providing a local mean-flow cue and improving oracle coverage, but it can also act as a ``tether'' that keeps samples conservative at the macroscopic level.

\paragraph{Residual diffusion as a structural fix.}
Residual decomposition separates responsibilities: the deterministic prior carries low-frequency scale and OD-driven displacement, while diffusion focuses on stochastic deviations around that anchor. This directly targets the failure mode by preventing the generative model from having to learn both physical scale and uncertainty from scratch.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
Our current pipeline is grid-based and uses a simple bbox projection for geographic visualization, without road-level map matching or explicit network constraints. Future work should incorporate road-graph structure, and explore adaptive conditioning mechanisms (e.g., learnable gating) to use the navigation field when helpful without over-constraining macroscopic motion. Finally, stronger conclusions require larger-scale evaluation and multi-seed replication under the same strict data contract.
