\subsection{Interpretation: why generative models trade mean error for coverage}
Our results illustrate a key methodological point: on multi-modal futures, minimizing point-wise mean error favors predicting a single conditional average, which can score well on ADE\_mean but does not represent the uncertainty of plausible routes. Diffusion-based generators explicitly model a conditional distribution and thus should be judged by (i) best-of-$K$ capability, and (ii) distribution-aware metrics. In this sense, the deterministic baseline is a strong reference for mean accuracy, while diffusion/physics models are evaluated for coverage and realistic variability.

\subsection{Shrinkage as a macro-level failure mode}
In Phase B v1.0, diffusion-based generators can exhibit a systematic under-estimation of macroscopic displacement (low MSD/Rog). This suggests insufficient \emph{directional persistence} (low-frequency structure): even when the instantaneous step magnitudes are non-trivial, frequent turning and cancellation reduce net displacement. A simple post-hoc velocity rescaling can partially correct amplitude but tends to amplify directional errors, indicating that shrinkage is not purely a scale calibration issue.

Our additional ablations indicate that naive training-time macro supervision is \emph{sensitive}: weak supervision may plateau, while overly strong supervision can over-compensate and distort typical samples. This motivates a structural remedy: in v1.1 we freeze a deterministic baseline as a low-frequency prior and let diffusion model only stochastic residuals. This decomposition makes the scale largely inherit from the prior, while preserving multi-modal diversity via residual sampling.

\subsection{Role of the navigation field (physics conditioning)}
The navigation field provides a local mean-flow prior estimated from historical data. This can improve directional guidance and best-of-$K$ (Physics outperforms data-only diffusion on ADE\_best/FDE\_best), which is consistent with the hypothesis that urban mobility is shaped by spatial constraints and recurrent flow patterns. At the same time, a mean-field prior can be conservative: it may regularize trajectories toward average behavior and thus does not automatically recover long-range displacement without additional macro supervision.

In the residual setting, the same tension remains: physics conditioning tends to improve oracle micro metrics but can stay slightly conservative in MSD/Rog. This is consistent with a ``mean-field tether'' interpretation: the nav\_field stabilizes direction but regularizes the generator toward typical local flow. A practical takeaway is to isolate variables: use residual decomposition to stabilize macroscopic scale first, then ablate the conditioning strength and sampling strategy for additional gains.

\subsection{Urban complex dynamics: what the model helps us understand}
From an urban analytics perspective, the value of a probabilistic trajectory generator is not limited to point-wise prediction. It enables:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Risk-aware forecasting}: sampling multiple plausible futures supports downstream planning under uncertainty (e.g., scenario analysis).
  \item \textbf{Spatial heterogeneity analysis}: map-space density and trajectory bundles reveal how mobility concentrates and diversifies across the city.
  \item \textbf{Physics-inspired priors}: the nav\_field can be interpreted as a coarse-grained flow field, linking micro trajectories to mesoscopic dynamics.
\end{itemize}
These aspects are aligned with the view of cities as complex dynamical systems, where uncertainty and variability are essential signals rather than noise to be suppressed.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}
Our current v1 pipeline is grid-based and uses a static nav\_patch, which is a simplifying approximation. For longer horizons, rolling (autoregressive) nav\_patch updates or road-level map matching would better respect spatial constraints. While residual diffusion substantially mitigates shrinkage, full distribution calibration (e.g., typical-sample realism vs. oracle best-of-$K$) remains an open problem for conditional mobility generation.

Promising next steps include (i) displacement-aware weighting for macro supervision so gradients are not diluted by near-stationary windows, (ii) multi-lag low-frequency constraints (a few intermediate lags in addition to end-point displacement), and (iii) faster, better-calibrated samplers (e.g., ODE solvers or distillation) to reduce inference cost without changing the learned scale.
