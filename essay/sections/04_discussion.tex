The discussion will focus on two questions that follow from the behavioral reference framing.

\paragraph{When is a behavioral reference useful?}
We will discuss what kinds of urban phenomena can plausibly be detected from route-choice residuals and what cannot. In particular, we distinguish between (i) connectivity or corridor effects that should appear in mobility traces and (ii) ruptures that may not strongly affect routing (or may be confounded by unobserved intent).

\paragraph{Maps and semantics as priors, not truth.}
We treat maps and semantics as informative context rather than as ground truth constraints. We will discuss robustness to imperfect maps and temporal mismatch in POI/imagery, and how soft priors help avoid binding the model's support to potentially incomplete external data.

\paragraph{Modeling as reference-building.}
We will reflect on the role of model failure modes (e.g., overly direct routes) as threats to the reference frame's validity. Our design choices (autoregressive corridor planning, deterministic execution, and validity-first gating) are justified by interpretability and auditability requirements rather than by purely predictive accuracy.
