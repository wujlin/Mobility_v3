\begin{abstract}
Route generation---sampling trip-level paths conditioned on origin, destination, and context---exhibits corridor-level multi-modality: distinct topological alternatives are far apart in coordinate space yet intent-equivalent.
End-to-end coordinate generators struggle under this multi-modality, exhibiting destructive averaging or long-horizon drift.
We propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a hierarchical decision--execution framework that makes corridor choice explicit on a road graph: a short-horizon autoregressive model samples sparse waypoint commitments conditioned on time and road hierarchy, then A* execution guarantees valid graph paths.
Diagnostic gates on WorldTrace segments show that (i) heuristic $K$-shortest candidates can miss ground-truth corridors (median Jaccard $\approx 0.21$), and (ii) time+tier context carries measurable signal for corridor variability (AUC $\approx 0.80$).
CascadeTraj achieves near-perfect route completion with improved corridor match over node-level autoregressive baselines.
\end{abstract}
