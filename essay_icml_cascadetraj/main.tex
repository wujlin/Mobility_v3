\begin{abstract}
``Realistic'' route generation requires more than geometric validity---it requires \emph{behavioral plausibility under temporal--spatial context}. Existing methods satisfy at most one: map-free generators drift off-road or collapse to straight lines; shortest-path routing is structurally valid but behaviorally implausible and context-insensitive. We propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a hierarchical framework that uses road topology as a structural mechanism and treats temporal--spatial semantics (time, road hierarchy, POI context) as the core signal that modulates corridor choice. The decision stage selects a corridor on the road graph conditioned on semantics and time; the execution stage generates a continuous trajectory within the corridor via residual diffusion. Our diagnostic finding is that map-free continuous generation fails at corridor-level convergence: waypoints remain far from ground-truth corridors even with soft road priors (median error 24.7 grid units with tier-road; 49.5 without). We define behavioral realism as \emph{context-conditioned diversity}: the same OD under different contexts should induce different corridor distributions. The contribution is not the architecture per se, but the framing: temporal--spatial semantics must enter at the decision level to produce meaningful behavioral diversity rather than indiscriminate randomness.
\end{abstract}
