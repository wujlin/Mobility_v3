Route generation is a fundamental primitive for mobility simulation, transportation planning, and urban computing \cite{}. Real-world routes are diverse given a fixed origin and destination (OD); they are by no means purely geometric curves nor deterministic shortest paths, because route selection may change based on assessments of the road and surrounding environment (i.e., urban semantics). However, the majority of existing works generate such paths by using road topology features as constraints. Specifically, road connectivity, intersection bottlenecks, and hierarchical attributes of the roads (e.g., highway, main road, and residential roads) are the most common elements working as constraints for route generation \cite{}. Although such works have the capability to generate routes to measure the overall traffic flow or trajectories in specific regions, this generation lacks an understanding of how routes diversify based on the urban physical and social environment, resulting in the emergence of different corridors. For example, in the real world, one may change the route to avoid traffic congestion and potential risks along the route, which potentially leads to the emergence of different corridors. However, previous works fail to provide insights into the emergence of corridors led by diverse routes and estimate the correct flow of each corridor. To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a hierarchical route generation framework that combines road network topology for structural realism with urban semantic features to diversify route generation and identify corridors. Within this framework, we divide route generation into two stages: 1) the decision stage and 2) the execution stage. Specifically, the decision stage selects a route on the road network graph conditioned on the urban semantic context, and the execution stage generates realistic trajectories within the selected route.
