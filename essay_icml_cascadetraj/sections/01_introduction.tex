Route generation is a primitive for mobility simulation, transportation planning, and urban computing [cite]. Real-world routes between a fixed origin (O) and destination (D) are diverse; they are by no means purely geometric curves nor deterministic shortest paths, because route selection may change based on assessments of the road and surrounding environment (i.e., urban semantics). However, the majority of existing works generate such paths using road topology features as constraints. Specifically, road connectivity, intersection bottlenecks, and hierarchical attributes of roads (e.g., highways, main roads, and residential roads) are the most common elements working as constraints for route generation [cite]. Even though such works have the capability to generate routes to measure overall traffic flow or trajectories in specific regions, these generations lack an understanding of how routes diversify based on the urban physical and social environment, resulting in the emergence of different corridors. For example, in the real world, one may change their route to avoid traffic congestion and potential risks along the way, which potentially leads to the emergence of different corridors. But previous works fail to provide insights into the emergence of corridors led by diverse routes and fail to estimate the correct flow of each corridor. To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a hierarchical route generation framework that combines road network topology for structural realism with urban semantic features to diversify route generation and identify corridors. Within this framework, we divide route generation into two stages: 1) the decision stage and 2) the execution stage. Specifically, the decision stage selects routes on the road network graph conditioned on the urban semantic context, and the execution stage generates realistic trajectories within the selected route.
