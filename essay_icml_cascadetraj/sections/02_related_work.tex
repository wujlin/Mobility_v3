\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Map-free trajectory generation.}
Early work on trajectory generation models continuous coordinates with recurrent networks, variational autoencoders, or adversarial objectives. Recent diffusion-based generators improve sample quality and diversity, but map-free generation remains fundamentally challenging for road-constrained mobility: in continuous space, the set of on-road trajectories occupies vanishing volume, and weak priors (e.g., distance-to-road penalties) often fail to enforce corridor-level realism at trip scale.

\paragraph{Map-aware routing and generative models.}
Classical routing algorithms (shortest path, $k$-shortest paths) provide strong structural validity by operating on a road graph. Learning-based approaches---including graph neural networks, inverse reinforcement learning, and imitation learning---learn costs or policies on road networks, but many focus on optimality or predictive accuracy rather than on controllable multi-modal generation. Recent generative models incorporate maps as constraints or conditioning signals; our work aligns with this direction but focuses on \emph{semantic control} of corridor choice rather than purely topological guidance.\footnote{TODO: add citations for recent road-network conditioned diffusion and hierarchical map-informed generators (e.g., arXiv:2507.13366; SIGSPATIAL'25 GCDM).}

\paragraph{Dialogue with map-conditioned generation.}
Recent map-conditioned generators (e.g., Map2Traj-style zero-shot generation; GTG-style cross-city generators) show that maps can greatly improve \emph{structural} realism and generalization. We agree: topology is a necessary structural prior. Our additional claim is that \emph{behavioral} realism requires more than topology: for the same road network, temporal--spatial semantics determine which corridor is preferred.

\paragraph{Hierarchical route choice.}
Empirically, trips often exhibit corridor-level multi-modality: a small number of plausible macro-routes exist between an OD pair, while local deviations occur within each corridor. Hierarchical formulations have been used in transportation (e.g., route--link decomposition) and in modern sequence modeling (coarse-to-fine generation). CascadeTraj operationalizes this hierarchy as a discrete decision on the road graph followed by continuous execution.

\paragraph{Urban semantics and temporal context.}
Route preferences depend on functional context---POI distributions, land use, road hierarchy, and time-of-day. Semantic features have been used as covariates for mobility prediction and as conditioning signals for generative models. We emphasize semantics as the \emph{behavioral} driver of corridor selection: given the same OD, semantics and time can shift the probability mass across corridors even when shortest-path geometry remains unchanged.

\paragraph{Foundation models and why mechanism matters.}
Recent ``trajectory foundation models'' (e.g., UniTraj/WorldTrace-style pretraining, TrajFM/TrajGPT variants) aim to learn region-independent representations from large-scale data. Our perspective is complementary: reconstruction-style pretraining optimizes statistical regularities of trajectories, but does not explicitly target \emph{context-dependent routing decisions}. The core behavioral phenomenon we study---the same OD yielding different corridor distributions at rush hour vs.\ midnight or under different semantic environments---requires modeling how temporal--spatial context \emph{modulates} corridor choice. CascadeTraj makes this modulation explicit by conditioning a discrete corridor selector on time and semantics.

\paragraph{Dialogue with road-constrained diffusion.}
Road-constrained diffusion models highlight that geometry constraints are necessary to avoid off-road artifacts. Our diagnostic results are consistent with this: even with soft road priors (tier-road), map-free decision waypoints remain far from corridors (Table~\ref{tab:wp-audit}), motivating a discrete corridor mechanism rather than purely continuous penalties.

\paragraph{Positioning with 2024--2025 trends.}
CascadeTraj sits at the intersection of several emerging trends:
\emph{coarse-to-fine/hierarchical generation} (decision--execution decomposition),
\emph{AR + diffusion hybrids} (discrete planning followed by continuous refinement),
\emph{map-conditioned generation} (road topology for structural realism),
and \emph{semantic/temporal conditioning} (context as a behavioral mechanism).
We do not claim that semantics are optional ``add-on features'': they define the behavioral realism target and provide the conditioning signals that make diversity meaningful rather than random.\footnote{TODO: add explicit citations and bib entries for UniTraj/WorldTrace (2025), GTG (AAAI 2025), Map2Traj (IJCAI 2025), CoDiffMob, Diff-RNTraj, and related map-conditioned diffusion papers.}
