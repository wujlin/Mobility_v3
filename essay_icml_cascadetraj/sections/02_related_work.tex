\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Map-free trajectory generation.}
Early work on trajectory generation models continuous coordinates with recurrent networks, variational autoencoders, or adversarial objectives~\citep{kong2023mobility_survey}.
Recent diffusion-based generators improve sample quality and diversity~\citep{zhu2023difftraj,zhang2025codiffmob}, but map-free generation remains fundamentally challenging for road-constrained mobility: in continuous space, the set of on-road trajectories occupies vanishing volume, and weak priors (e.g., distance-to-road penalties) often fail to enforce corridor-level realism at trip scale.
Our work differs by diagnosing this failure at the \emph{corridor} scale and motivating a discrete decision formulation rather than stronger continuous penalties.

\paragraph{Road-constrained diffusion and the measure-zero issue.}
Recent road-constrained diffusion work~\citep{wei2024diffrntraj,zhu2024controltraj,guo2025cardiff} highlights that geometry constraints are necessary to avoid off-road artifacts.
Our results are consistent with this perspective, and further show that even soft road priors (tier-road) leave decision waypoints far from GT corridors (Table~\ref{tab:wp-audit}).
Our work differs by connecting the measure-zero issue to \emph{corridor-level} decision making and motivating corridor selection as a discrete structural mechanism.

\paragraph{Map-conditioned generation and cross-city generalization.}
Map-conditioned generators such as Map2Traj~\citep{tao2025map2traj} demonstrate that road topology can substantially improve \emph{structural} realism and even enable strong zero-shot transfer.
Cross-city generators such as GTG~\citep{wang2025gtg} show that mobility patterns can be transferable across regions.
We agree with the structural and transfer insights, but argue that these approaches leave an essential question unanswered: \emph{what modulates corridor choice under fixed OD and topology?}
Our answer is temporal--spatial semantics; our work differs by making this modulation explicit via corridor selection conditioned on time, road hierarchy, and POI context.

\paragraph{Hierarchical route choice.}
Empirically, trips often exhibit corridor-level multi-modality: a small number of plausible macro-routes exist between an OD pair, while local deviations occur within each corridor.
Hierarchical formulations have been used in transportation (e.g., route--link decomposition) and in modern sequence modeling (coarse-to-fine generation)~\citep{guo2025cardiff,rao2025seed}.
Our work differs by instantiating the hierarchy on a road graph corridor space and separating corridor selection from continuous execution.

\paragraph{Urban semantics and temporal context.}
Route preferences depend on functional context---POI distributions, land use, road hierarchy, and time-of-day.
Semantic/temporal conditioning has been explored in recent mobility generation models~\citep{cao2025hoser,su2024uen,zhang2025codiffmob}.
Our key positioning is that semantics are not auxiliary features: they define behavioral realism by inducing \emph{context-conditioned} corridor distribution shifts under the same OD and topology.

\paragraph{Foundation models and why mechanism matters.}
Trajectory foundation models such as UniTraj~\citep{zhu2025unitraj} and TrajFM~\citep{lin2024trajfm} aim to learn region-independent representations from large-scale data via masking-and-recovery style objectives.
Our perspective is complementary: reconstruction optimizes trajectory-level statistical regularities, but corridor choice is a \emph{decision problem}.
A model may reconstruct coordinates accurately yet fail to capture that the same OD should route through different corridors under different temporal--spatial contexts.
Our work differs by treating corridor selection as a discrete decision conditioned on semantics/time, targeting the behavioral mechanism rather than only reconstruction fidelity.
