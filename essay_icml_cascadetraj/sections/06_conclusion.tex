We presented a new narrative for trip-scale route generation: structural realism requires explicit road topology, while behavioral fidelity requires semantic and temporal context. Our diagnostic results show that map-free continuous generation does not reliably converge to road corridors at trip scale, even with soft road-proximity priors, motivating a discrete corridor decision on the road graph.

CascadeTraj operationalizes this insight with a decision--execution decomposition: corridor selection on a road graph followed by continuous trajectory generation conditioned on the selected corridor. Ongoing work is integrating the corridor selector end-to-end and quantifying semantic control (POI, road hierarchy, time-of-day) beyond shortest-path routing.

Limitations include dependence on road-graph quality and the need for robust corridor labeling from trajectories. Future work will expand semantic features (including time-dynamic POI activity) and evaluate cross-city transfer under consistent corridor definitions.
