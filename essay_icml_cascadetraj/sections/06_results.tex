\subsection{Gate 1: Heuristic Candidates Have Low Corridor Coverage}

\paragraph{Ground truth is well aligned to the road graph.}
On Detroit segments, ground-truth points are typically close to the road graph: 94\% of GT points are within 1 grid cell of a road edge (p90 distance $<1$).
This confirms that low corridor match is not driven by GT/map misalignment.

\paragraph{$K$-shortest candidates miss many ground-truth corridors.}
Despite good GT alignment, heuristic candidate sets exhibit low corridor coverage.
With $K=20$ shortest-path variants, the median best-of-$K$ edge Jaccard match to GT is only $\approx 0.21$ (\cref{tab:candidate-coverage}), indicating that ``classify among $K$'' is an unreliable surrogate for learning the corridor distribution.
This motivates learning structured corridor commitments rather than depending on a fixed candidate set.

\subsection{Gate 2: Context Carries Measurable Corridor-Level Signal}

\paragraph{AUC on corridor clusters.}
Using OD buckets and edge-Jaccard clustering within each bucket, we form a corridor-label prediction task.
Across Detroit and Columbus, time+tier features achieve a clear above-chance signal (mean AUC $\approx 0.80$ over 22 usable OD groups).
Per-city, Columbus exhibits a stronger signal (mean AUC $\approx 0.84$ over 18 groups) while Detroit is weaker but still above chance (mean AUC $\approx 0.61$ over 4 groups), highlighting the role of data volume and OD repetition (\cref{tab:semantic-gate}).

\subsection{Corridor Generation: Node AR vs.\ Waypoint AR + A*}

\paragraph{Node-level AR is fragile at long horizons.}
We train a next-node predictor on graph transitions (validation accuracy $\approx 0.76$), but long-horizon sampling remains challenging: for $K=20$ samples, best-of-$K$ corridor match is modest ($J_{\mathrm{best}}$ mean $\approx 0.20$) and success depends strongly on route length.
This reflects compounding error over hundreds of steps.

\paragraph{Waypoint AR + A* reduces decision horizon.}
Our waypoint AR model predicts a 4-step waypoint commitment in a coarse $32\times 32$ bin space (best validation accuracy $\approx 0.34$).
Executing sampled waypoints with A* guarantees graph-validity for successful samples.
In the multi-city setting, we found that naive bin-to-node instantiation can fail by mixing nodes across disjoint city subgraphs that share a normalized grid coordinate system.
After enforcing city-consistent decoding and a reachability-friendly bin instantiation heuristic (major-road preference + OD-aligned direction), waypoint AR + A* achieves near-perfect completion (\cref{tab:main-results}).

\paragraph{Ceiling analysis.}
An oracle experiment that replaces predicted bins with GT-derived bins shows only a small gain in corridor match, indicating that the remaining bottleneck is bin granularity rather than the bin predictor.
Finer bins or adaptive waypoint counts are promising directions for future work.
