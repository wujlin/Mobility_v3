\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon mobility modeling is increasingly important for simulation and generative urban agents, yet most learning methods target short-horizon trajectory forecasting.
We study \emph{route generation}: sampling an entire path for a trip conditioned on an origin, a destination, and time/context.
Route generation is fundamentally multi-modal at the corridor level: distinct topological alternatives can be geometrically far apart while remaining intent-equivalent.
Through diagnostic experiments on large-scale mobility traces, we identify two characteristic failures of end-to-end coordinate generators under this multi-modality: deterministic regression collapses to destructive averages, while stochastic generation can avoid hard collapse yet blurs corridor alternatives through mode interference.
We propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a decision--execution generator that first samples a sparse waypoint skeleton as an explicit topological commitment, then refines it with a continuous execution model.
We further find that naive composition can overwrite corridor commitments; treating execution as a weak residual refinement preserves corridor coverage while still capturing local geometry.
The framework supports soft map priors (e.g., road-proximity fields) for feasibility, and admits additional spatial covariates (e.g., POI/land-use or census attributes) only as optional ablations for extensibility.
\end{abstract}
