\paragraph{From short-horizon forecasting to route generation.}
Urban mobility traces record how people realize intent through space: not only \emph{where} they go, but \emph{how} they get there.
Recent progress in sequence models and diffusion models \citep{vaswani2017attention,ho2020ddpm} has made short-horizon trajectory prediction increasingly accurate.
Yet many practical and scientific tasks require a different capability: \emph{route generation} over a full trip.
Given an origin--destination intent and context (e.g., departure time and neighborhood semantics), a route generator must sample an entire path that is realistic, feasible, and diverse.

\paragraph{Why long-horizon route generation fails.}
We observe two recurrent failure modes when extending generative modeling from seconds to full trips.
First, intent can drift: purely autoregressive rollouts accumulate small errors and become globally inconsistent with the destination.
Second, feasibility can break: when emphasizing diversity (e.g., via latent-variable models \citep{kingma2014auto} or standard diffusion), generators often hallucinate physically invalid paths that cut through obstacles or deviate systematically from the road network---especially when explicit map supervision is absent or unreliable.

\paragraph{Root cause: topological multi-modality and destructive averaging.}
The core difficulty is \emph{topological multi-modality}.
Even for the same origin and destination, multiple corridor-level choices can be equally reasonable.
End-to-end coordinate generators trained with likelihood or Euclidean losses therefore face a destructive ``averaging'' pressure: the mean of several feasible routes is often an infeasible straight line.
This suggests that route generation should be modeled as a \emph{decision--execution} process rather than a single homogeneous sequence problem.

\paragraph{A diagnostic baseline: mode interference in continuous output space.}
We first provide a diagnostic experiment (Fig.~\ref{fig:mode-collapse}) using an end-to-end generative baseline trained under the same data and conditioning.
To avoid cherry-picking, we fix diagnostic OD cases via a ground-truth corridor audit (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).
We observe a characteristic collapse: samples either concentrate around overly direct routes or blur distinct corridor-level alternatives into implausible averages.
This supports the hypothesis that the bottleneck is not simply model capacity, but \emph{mode interference} when corridor-level decisions are represented only implicitly in a continuous coordinate output space.

\paragraph{CascadeTraj: bridging semantic intent and physical execution.}
We propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a cascaded generative framework for route generation.
At the \emph{decision} stage, the model samples a sparse route skeleton (e.g., waypoints) that represents discrete corridor-level alternatives and preserves long-horizon intent consistency.
At the \emph{execution} stage, the model generates a full-resolution continuous route conditioned on the skeleton, capturing local geometry and motion patterns.
This decomposition turns a brittle one-shot generation problem into two conditional problems with clearer responsibilities: (i) committing to a coherent topological plan, and (ii) executing a physically plausible realization.

\paragraph{Soft priors and optional census guidance.}
To encourage feasibility without collapsing support, we incorporate map information as \emph{soft} priors (e.g., road-proximity fields) rather than hard output masking.
We also support injecting census-derived spatial covariates as optional semantic guidance signals that can steer route preferences without making population/attribute synthesis the main objective of this paper.
While macro-level statistics can provide useful priors for controllability, this paper focuses on the generative architecture for the \emph{route itself}; census integration is explored only as an ablation to demonstrate extensibility.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Finding 1 (Diagnosis):} we provide evidence that topological multi-modality induces mode interference and destructive averaging in end-to-end long-horizon route generators, which cannot be fixed by naively scaling continuous coordinate models.
  \item \textbf{Finding 2 (Solution):} we show that a decision--execution decomposition, where a discrete topological commitment (waypoint skeleton) precedes continuous generation, breaks the destructive averaging trap and enables corridor-level diversity.
  \item \textbf{Robustness:} we incorporate map information as \emph{soft} priors (instead of hard masking) to encourage feasibility without truncating support, and treat additional macro semantics (e.g., census covariates) strictly as optional ablations for extensibility.
\end{itemize}
