\begin{abstract}
Urban route generation---sampling trip-level paths on a road network conditioned on origin, destination, and context---exhibits \emph{corridor-level multi-modality}: for a given origin-destination pair, drivers choose among topologically distinct corridors that are far apart on the road graph yet intent-equivalent.
Sequence-level autoregressive models make only local next-step decisions and lack the capacity for such global corridor commitment; on a large-scale taxi dataset they reach destinations in fewer than 24\% of attempts.
CascadeTraj factors route generation into corridor commitment and constrained local execution.
An information-bottlenecked latent space compresses variable-length way-level route encodings into a 64-dimensional corridor identity, and a conditional flow model samples from this space given trip context.
The bottleneck dimension exhibits a capacity sweet spot: too low and decoder precision degrades gracefully; too high and the flow model collapses to mode averaging---a 25-percentage-point drop in arrival rate.
On 108K Porto taxi routes, CascadeTraj achieves 78.3\% arrival rate ($3.4\times$ the best autoregressive baseline) and $4.2\times$ higher corridor coverage, while simultaneously producing greater route diversity.
\end{abstract}
