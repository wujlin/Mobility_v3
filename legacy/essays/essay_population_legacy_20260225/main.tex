\begin{abstract}
Long-horizon mobility modeling is increasingly important for simulation and generative urban agents, yet most learning methods target short-horizon trajectory forecasting.
We study \emph{route generation}: sampling an entire trip-level path conditioned on an origin, a destination, and time/context.
Route generation is fundamentally multi-modal at the corridor level: distinct topological alternatives can be far apart while remaining intent-equivalent.
End-to-end coordinate generators struggle under this multi-modality, exhibiting destructive averaging or long-horizon drift.
We propose \textbf{CascadeTraj}, a hierarchical decision--execution route generator that makes corridor choice explicit on a road graph.
The decision stage samples a sparse waypoint commitment with a short-horizon autoregressive model conditioned on time and road hierarchy; the execution stage connects waypoints with A* to guarantee valid graph paths (and admits optional continuous refinement).
We provide diagnostic gates showing (i) heuristic candidate sets (e.g., $K$-shortest paths) can have low corridor coverage, motivating learned structured decisions, and (ii) temporal and road-tier context carries measurable signal for corridor-level variability in multi-modal OD groups.
\end{abstract}
