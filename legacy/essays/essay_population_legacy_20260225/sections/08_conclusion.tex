We study trip-level route generation and highlight corridor-level multi-modality as a structural challenge for end-to-end coordinate generators and candidate-set formulations.
We propose CascadeTraj as a graph-based decision--execution framework: a short-horizon waypoint autoregressive model samples corridor commitments conditioned on time and road hierarchy, and A* executes these commitments into valid graph paths.
Our gates provide evidence that (i) heuristic candidate sets can have low corridor coverage, and (ii) time+tier context carries measurable signal for corridor variability in multi-modal OD groups.
Future work will incorporate reachability-aware decoding, scale to more cities and full WorldTrace, and connect corridor generation to continuous executors for realistic within-corridor trajectories.
